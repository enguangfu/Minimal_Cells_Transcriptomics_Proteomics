\section{Discussion}

Full-length sequencing produced an operon-resolved transcriptome of \syn~and matched transcriptome and proteome measurements in both \syn~and \syna, and showed that transcription is the primary layer shaping protein output, with RNA processing and genome minimization as modifiers.

The transcriptome proved richer than the reduced gene count implies.
We mapped 459 operons whose full-length isoforms carried matched promoters and terminators, and their primary transcripts were pervasively reshaped, with markedly more 3$'$ than 5$'$ erosion.
We interpret this asymmetry as a 3$'\rightarrow$5$'$ exonucleolytic clearance bottleneck rather than biased endonucleolytic cutting, a hypothesis direct cleavage-site mapping will test.

Across \syn, transcript level was the dominant determinant of protein abundance, with translation-elongation features explaining part of the residual variance.
The antisense and intergenic transcription we detected traced largely to inherited mis-annotation and synthetic-construction artifacts, and those regions were among the first removed during minimization to \syna.

Minimization was structurally conservative yet functionally transformative.
It preserved gene order and excised whole operons, decapitating only a few retained operons by deleting their own promoters, with the nucleoid protein HupA the clearest case.
The retained transcriptome reallocated toward the translation machinery, especially a single 11~kb ribosomal-protein operon tripling its mRNA-pool share, while RNA polymerase and central-metabolic enzymes declined and the RNA degradosome rose, coherent with \syna's longer cell cycle.
Two non-exclusive effects could underlie this operon's rise: the tRNA operon that minimization brought adjacent to it may raise the local availability of RNA polymerase without read-through, and the loss of the nucleoid protein HupA may have shifted genome-wide supercoiling toward strong promoters.
Whether imbalanced ribosomal-protein synthesis burdens assembly remains speculative.

Several limitations remain.
The homology-mapped processing sites await direct cleavage-site mapping in \syn, since conserved ribonucleases need not act on conserved RNA structures; the truncated \syna~nanopore reads leave operon-level changes to be confirmed by PacBio; expression was framed as mRNA-pool share and excludes rRNA; and ribosomal and membrane-protein abundances are the least reliable, which ribosome profiling could resolve.
Together these data establish a gene-coexpression foundation for incorporation into a four-dimensional whole-cell model of the minimal cell~\cite{thornburg_bringing_2026}.
