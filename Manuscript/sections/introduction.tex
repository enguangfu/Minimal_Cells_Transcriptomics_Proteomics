\section{Introduction}

Bacterial genes are transcribed in operons~\cite{jacob_genetic_1961} and their transcripts are further shaped by ribonucleolytic processing~\cite{taggart_quantitative_2021}, organizing the transcriptome above the level of the single gene.
\jsyn~(\syn) and its minimized derivative \jsyna~(\syna), genetically modified and reduced versions of the naturally occurring bacterium \mmy~\cite{gibson_creation_2010, hutchison_design_2016}, are landmark platforms for defining the minimal requirements of cellular life, yet they have been characterized chiefly through genome design and protein essentiality~\cite{breuer_essential_2019}, leaving how their genes are organized and processed at the RNA level largely uncharted.
The two cells also differ in phenotype: \syna~divides more slowly~\cite{pelletier_genetic_2021}, packs a denser cytosol ~\cite{gilbert_generating_2021}, and lacks the persistent chromosome contacts, the last linked to its low level of the nucleoid-associated protein HupA~\cite{gilbert_generating_2021}.

Three questions follow.
How is gene expression organized across a synthetic genome, from operon boundaries and transcription signals to downstream RNA processing?
Does transcription predict the proteome once the genetic background is stripped toward minimal complexity?
And what does genome minimization itself do to the expression program, beyond deleting genes?

Resolving how genes are co-expressed requires reading transcripts at full length: short-read Illumina sequencing quantifies transcript abundance robustly but fragments RNA and cannot show which genes share a transcript~\cite{stark_rna_2019}, whereas long-read sequencing recovers full-length molecules and so exposes operon co-transcription, processing isoforms, and antisense or intergenic transcription~\cite{yan_smrt-cappable-seq_2018, grunberger_nanopore_2022}.
We therefore captured full-length transcripts with PacBio in \syn~and Oxford Nanopore in \syna~, using Illumina short reads as the standard for transcript quantification, and combined these with matched proteomics to build a genome-wide, operon-resolved map of transcription, RNA processing, and translation for both organisms and to compare how halving the genome reshaped it.
This atlas supplies the RNA-level foundation at gene co-expression and quantification that whole-cell modeling of the minimal cell has so far lacked~\cite{thornburg_fundamental_2022, thornburg_bringing_2026}.
