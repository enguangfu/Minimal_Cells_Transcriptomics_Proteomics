\section{Abstract}

\jsyn~(\syn) and its minimized derivative \jsyna~(\syna), genetically modified and reduced versions of the naturally occurring bacterium \mmy, are landmark platforms for defining the gene set required for life, yet how their genomes are expressed at the RNA level remains uncharted.
We combined full-length long-read RNA sequencing, short-read quantification, and matched proteomics to map transcription, RNA processing, and translation across both cells.
Long reads resolved 459 operons (911 genes) in \syn~with matched promoters and terminators, whose transcripts were pervasively reshaped by 3$'$-biased processing.
Transcript level was the dominant determinant of protein abundance, and the antisense and intergenic transcription complicating \syn~traced to inherited mis-annotation and synthetic artifacts that minimization preferentially removed.
Halving the genome disrupted a few essential-gene promoters, notably the nucleoid protein HupA, and reallocated the transcriptome toward translation, especially a 21-gene ribosomal-protein operon, while RNA polymerase and central metabolism declined and the degradosome rose, coherent with \syna's slower growth.
These data provide the RNA-level foundation for whole-cell modeling of the minimal cell.
