\subsubsection{Ribonucleases in Syn1 and Syn3A}

The ribonuclease complement of \syn~was read from the genome annotation and assigned activities from the characterised \textit{Bacillus subtilis} orthologues.
Both \syn~and \syna~encode the same compact set: the endoribonucleases RNase~III, RNase~Y and RNase~M5, the 5$'\rightarrow$3$'$ exoribonucleases RNase~J1 and RNase~J2, the 3$'\rightarrow$5$'$ exoribonucleases RNase~R and YhaM, and the \textit{trans}-translation factors tmRNA and SmpB (Table~\ref{tbl:rnase_syn1_syn3a})~\cite{durand_rnases_2018, redko_minimal_2013}.
Per-cell abundances were the proteomic copy numbers (Section on proteomics), and substrate signatures were taken from the corresponding \textit{B. subtilis} enzymes.

\subsubsection{RNA-processing endpoint analysis}

Post-transcriptional processing was quantified from the clustered PacBio isoforms.
Isoforms supported by at least ten reads were retained, giving 20{,}885 isoforms.
A per-strand mask of protein-coding gene bodies was built from the \syn~GFF3 annotation, and the 5$'$ and the 3$'$ genomic endpoint of each isoform was labelled intragenic when it fell inside a gene body and intergenic otherwise.
Each isoform was assigned to one of four end-context categories --- unprocessed (both ends intergenic), 5$'$-eroded only, 3$'$-eroded only, or both-eroded --- and the composition was tabulated both by isoform count and by read.
The erosion asymmetry was summarised as the ratio of the read weight carried by 5$'$-intragenic to that carried by 3$'$-intragenic isoforms.
Independently, every annotated start and stop codon fully contained within an isoform was counted, and a reading frame that contained its start codon but not its stop codon was scored as 3$'$-truncated.
For the single-gene panels, the isoforms contained within an operon were coloured by end-context and ordered into the erosion ladder; for the ATP-synthase panel the member isoforms of the two segmented operons were coloured by transcription block.
Analyses were run in Python 3.11 with pandas 2.1, NumPy 1.26 and SciPy 1.11, and figures were drawn with Matplotlib 3.8.

\subsubsection{Mapping B. subtilis Endoribonuclease Cleavage Sites onto Syn1}

The experimentally mapped RNase~III and RNase~Y cleavage sites of \textit{B. subtilis}~\cite{taggart_high-resolution_2025} were transferred onto \syn~by protein homology to ask whether the conserved endoribonucleases act on conserved RNA structures.
Orthologues were defined as reciprocal best BLASTP (BLAST+) hits between the two proteomes ($\geq$25\% identity and $\geq$50\% coverage in both directions, e-value $\leq 10^{-5}$, bit score $\geq$50); 18 \syn~genes carried a homology-supported RNase~III site.
Because RNA secondary structure need not be conserved even at high protein identity, whether each conserved endoribonuclease still acts on a conserved structure was tested directly from the \syn~fold rather than inferred from sequence.
Each \textit{B. subtilis} cleavage site was projected onto its \syn~orthologue by transcript fraction to give the homologous position; the entire \syn~gene was then folded once (RNAfold, ViennaRNA 2.6.4, default parameters), and the base-pairing at the projected position was read directly from that single structure without re-folding.
RNase~III cuts both strands of one helix, so a conserved site was scored only when the two staggered cut sites of a gene faced each other across one duplex, that is when the base-pairing partner of each cut lay within four nucleotides of the other cut, the geometry of the 2-nt 3$'$ overhang.
This criterion was calibrated on \textit{B. subtilis} \gene{atpA}{0792}, whose two sites pair about 105~nt apart across a long central stem yet face with a cross-distance of 3~nt.
By this test none of the 18 \syn~genes reproduced a clean cross-paired RNase~III duplex at the homologous position: the two cut sites of \gene{atpA}{0792}, for example, folded into separate local stem-loops rather than the single extended stem of \textit{B. subtilis} (cross-distance 104~nt), indicating that the cleaved RNA structures, unlike the enzymes that cut them, are not conserved.
The homology-mapped cleavage sites were therefore reported as projected positions and displayed on the local \syn~secondary structure, where each falls within a stem (Fig.~\ref{fig:rnase}g).
RNase~Y sites were projected analogously and scored for a stable structure immediately downstream of the cut, matching the enzyme's single-stranded preference.
