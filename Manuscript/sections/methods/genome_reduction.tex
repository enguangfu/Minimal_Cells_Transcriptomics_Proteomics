The transcriptional consequences of minimization from \jsyn~to \jsyna~were dissected with a fixed pipeline that first localized every deletion on a common coordinate frame, then propagated each deletion through \syn's operon structure, and finally tested the predicted regulatory effects against \syna~reads.  % pipeline: Genome_Reduction/ scripts 01 to 10, run in numeric order
All coordinates were handled as 0-based half-open intervals on the circular chromosome, with neighbor and intergenic-distance calculations wrapping at the origin.

\subsubsection{Whole-genome alignment of Syn3A onto Syn1.0 and deletion extraction}

The \syna~genome (CP016816.2, 543{,}379~bp) was aligned onto the \syn~genome (CP002027.1, 1{,}078{,}809~bp) to recast minimization as a set of discrete, locatable deletions on a single reference frame.  % scripts: 01_align.sh -> 02_analyze.py -> 03_visualize.py
Alignment used \texttt{nucmer} from MUMmer v3.23~\cite{kurtz_versatile_2004} in \texttt{-{}-maxmatch} mode (minimum cluster length 100~bp, minimum exact seed 20~bp), and the delta was reduced to a single best one-to-one mapping with \texttt{delta-filter} (\texttt{-1}; $\geq 95\%$ identity, $\geq 100$~bp).
Single-base differences and rearrangement breakpoints were tabulated with \texttt{dnadiff} and \texttt{show-snps}.
Deletions were defined as \syn~intervals covered by no one-to-one alignment block: the blocks were merged across gaps $\leq 50$~bp and complemented against the \syn~genome with BEDTools v2.30.0~\cite{quinlan_bedtools_2010}, and complement intervals shorter than 50~bp were discarded as boundary noise.
This yielded the authoritative set of 95 deletions ($\geq 50$~bp) removing 536{,}543~bp ($\sim$536~kb) of \syn~sequence (\texttt{syn1\_deleted\_regions.bed}), each annotated with the \syn~genes it overlapped by intersection against the gene annotation (\texttt{syn1.genes.gff3}).
Across the retained backbone the two genomes were 99.90\% identical, differing by only 36 single-nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) and 12 short insertions or deletions (indels), with no inversions and no translocations, and gene order was preserved apart from a single relocated block (\gene{lap}{0154}), so expression differences at retained genes are not attributable to sequence divergence.
Locus tags correspond one-to-one between organisms with the numeric suffix preserved (\locus{NNNN} $\leftrightarrow$ \locusA{NNNN}), which was used throughout to map \syn~genes to their \syna~orthologs.

\subsubsection{Overlaying deletions onto Syn1.0 operons}

Each of the 95 deletions was intersected at single-base resolution with the 459~\syn~operons called from PacBio long reads (against \texttt{operons.candidate\_blocks.tsv}).  % script: 04_deletion_overlaid_operon.py
Every operon was described along two orthogonal axes.
The truncation axis classified each overlapping deletion on its own as \texttt{fully\_deleted} (both operon boundaries removed), \texttt{5'\_truncation\_gene} or \texttt{5'\_truncation\_UTR}, \texttt{3'\_truncation\_gene} or \texttt{3'\_truncation\_UTR} (a boundary cut that reached a sense-gene coding sequence, CDS, or stopped in the untranslated region, UTR, respectively), or \texttt{intra\_truncated} (lying strictly inside the operon, neither boundary touched).
The 5$'$ versus 3$'$ assignment was strand-aware, and an operon hit by more than one deletion took the sorted union of its per-hit cases.
The gene-deletion axis classified the operon by which of its sense genes were lost as \texttt{intact}, \texttt{all\_deleted}, \texttt{leading\_deleted}, \texttt{lagging\_deleted}, or \texttt{intra\_deleted}.
The resulting per-operon table (sheet Operon\_classification of the genome-reduction supplementary workbook \texttt{genome\_reduction.xlsx}) was the structural input to the junction, co-transcription, and gene-impact analyses below.

\subsubsection{Deletion-junction taxonomy}

Every deletion was reframed as a junction between the two retained operon fragments it joins.  % script: 05_deletion_junction.py
\texttt{operon\_L} was the \syn~operon of the nearest \syna-retained gene to the left of the deletion and \texttt{operon\_R} the nearest retained gene to the right; genes absent from \syna~were skipped, and a gene covered only in antisense by an operon was assigned to that operon and treated as transcribed on the operon's strand.
The relative orientation of the two flanking operons defined \texttt{strand\_relationship}: \texttt{intra\_operon} (\texttt{operon\_L} $=$ \texttt{operon\_R}, the deletion internal to one operon), \texttt{tandem} (the two operons co-oriented, the only configuration in which one transcript can run across the junction), \texttt{convergent} (\texttt{operon\_L} on $+$ and \texttt{operon\_R} on $-$, their terminators facing the gap), and \texttt{divergent} (\texttt{operon\_L} on $-$ and \texttt{operon\_R} on $+$, their promoters facing the gap).
At a tandem junction only two regulators decide the outcome, the upstream operon's terminator (its 3$'$ end facing the gap) and the downstream operon's promoter (its 5$'$ end facing the gap), each scored as lost when its coordinate fell inside the deletion.
The four combinations defined \texttt{junction\_type}: \texttt{clean\_excision} (both regulators kept, whole operon(s) excised between independent neighbors), \texttt{decapitation} (downstream promoter lost, predicting an expression drop), \texttt{readthrough\_extension} (upstream terminator lost, predicting additive read-in), and \texttt{fusion} (both lost, predicting a single chimeric transcription unit).
A consistency check against the operon-overlay classification was run on every execution, mapping each lost regulator to its expected truncation side (a lost promoter to a 5$'$ truncation, a lost terminator to a 3$'$ truncation) and flagging disagreements (sheet Deletions of \texttt{genome\_reduction.xlsx}).

\subsubsection{Co-transcription tests from Syn3A reads}

Whether a same-strand gene pair was still co-transcribed in \syna~was tested directly from reads, using the \syna~ONT direct-RNA alignment and the Illumina per-strand sequencing depth (pysam v0.22.1, matching-strand depth throughout).  % shared primitives: coexpression_common.py
A pair passed the strict, spanning test when at least one primary ONT read on the matching strand fully enclosed both CDSs and the intergenic region between them.
A pair passed the loose, bridging test when the number of ONT reads that overlapped each CDS by $\geq 50$~bp and covered the intergenic gap reached $\max(1,\, \min(10,\, 0.20 \times d_{\min}))$, where $d_{\min}$ is the lower of the two mean ONT gene depths, and when the intergenic gap was either shorter than 10~bp or carried mean Illumina depth $\geq 20\%$ of the lower flanking-gene Illumina depth.
Operon-internal co-transcription walked every consecutive retained sense-gene pair within an operon and called the operon preserved only when all of its pairs passed; the operons tested were routed from the junction table into \texttt{pristine} controls (gene-deletion pattern \texttt{intact} and flanking no junction, so the reduction never touched them) and \texttt{intra\_operon} operons (a deletion removed interior genes).  % script: 06_single_operon_coexpression.py
Cross-junction co-transcription tested the newly adjacent gene pair at each inter-operon junction, each gene mapped to its \syna~ortholog by locus suffix, stratified by \texttt{junction\_type}: \texttt{fusion} junctions were predicted to co-transcribe and \texttt{clean\_excision} junctions served as the negative control, while \texttt{convergent} and \texttt{divergent} junctions lie on opposite strands and were reported as structural negatives.  % script: 07_operon_pair_coexpression.py
Because \syna~ONT depth is low, most positive calls rested on the loose bridging criterion rather than on fully spanning reads (sheet Coexpression of \texttt{genome\_reduction.xlsx}).

\subsubsection{Per-gene transcriptional-impact classification}

Each \syn~gene retained in \syna~(no base of its CDS overlapping any deletion) was annotated with its same-strand transcription-direction neighbors, its clockwise and counter-clockwise neighbors in both genomes, and the length of contiguous unaltered context flanking it (\syn~frame, circular).  % script: 08_delete_gene.py
Each retained gene was then assigned a \texttt{gene\_impact\_class} describing how the reduction changed the source of its transcription, integrating the operon-overlay, junction, and co-transcription results.  % integrates 04 (overlay), 05 (junctions), 06/07 (co-transcription)
Classes were assigned by precedence, the first matching rule winning: \texttt{promoter\_lost} (the operon lost its 5$'$ promoter with no replacement, assigned at operon level to all of its retained genes), \texttt{promoter\_disconnected} (the promoter was kept but the operon split internally, separating downstream genes from it), \texttt{new\_promoter\_fusion} (the operon lost its own promoter but fused to the upstream operon's promoter, confirmed by cross-junction co-transcription), \texttt{readthrough\_exposed} (the gene kept its promoter while the upstream operon lost its terminator), \texttt{promoter\_proximity\_changed} (an intra-operon deletion removed interior genes while the operon stayed co-transcribed), \texttt{context\_only} (the operon's own 3$'$ structure was truncated but its promoter and upstream context were intact), and \texttt{unaffected} (the operon's own structure was intact, including operons that merely neighbor a deletion).
This classification is purely structural; the corresponding changes in transcript and protein abundance were quantified separately (sheet Gene\_table of \texttt{genome\_reduction.xlsx}, with the abundance comparison described in the following subsection).

\subsubsection{Relative-abundance normalization and the paired expression-change table}

Transcript and protein abundances were compared between the two organisms after a normalization that removes the artefact of the reduced gene complement.
TPM and iPM are both relative units that each sum to $10^{6}$ within an organism, so when several hundred genes are absent from \syna~the same fixed pool is divided among fewer genes and every retained gene's value is mechanically inflated, leaving a naive \syna/\syn~ratio well above 1 even for genes whose true abundance is unchanged.
To remove this baseline, each gene's value was expressed relative to the per-gene mean of its own organism and subset, $\mathrm{rel}_i = v_i / \bar{v}$, where $\bar{v}$ is the mean over the genes detected in that subset (coding or non-coding), so that the average gene equals 1 in both organisms and an unchanged gene gives a fold change of $\sim$1 regardless of the gene-count difference.
The same normalization was applied independently to the transcriptome and the proteome, yielding relTPM and relIPM, and two change metrics were derived for each layer: a fold change, the \syna~relative value divided by the \syn~relative value, and an absolute change, their difference, which is robust where a denominator is small.
Illumina TPM was used for both organisms because ONT direct-RNA is unreliable for quantification, with ONT retained only for quality-control correlation against the Illumina tracks; the non-coding subset (rRNA and tRNA) was the sole exception and took \syna~ONT TPM, since the Ribo-Zero Illumina library detects none of these species.
The proteome layer used the mean-normalized iPM from the relative protein quantification described above, carrying the same fold-change and absolute-change definitions.
The transcriptome and proteome layers were joined by locus number into a single paired table (compiled into sheet Gene\_table of \texttt{genome\_reduction.xlsx}), from which the protein-specific figures and outlier reports were generated separately.  % scripts: 09_Compare_RNA_Protein.py builds the table + RNA figures; 10_Compare_Ptn.py reads it for the protein figures

\subsubsection{Deleted-gene occupancy and function-category reallocation}

The expression capacity freed by minimization was quantified as the share of \syn's transcriptome and proteome carried by loci absent from \syna, using raw (un-normalized) \syn~TPM and iPM so the values read as fractions of the whole, with the deleted loci partitioned by RNA type (sheet Summary\_stats of \texttt{genome\_reduction.xlsx}).
The reorganization of the retained pool was then analyzed by curated function, joining coding genes to their Primary, Secondary, and Tertiary function annotation by locus number (\texttt{syn3A\_proteome\_annotated.xlsx}).
Cross-organism category shares require an additional correction, because \syn's full-pool mean is diluted by the deleted genes and therefore inflates the relative values of retained genes relative to \syna, which consists entirely of retained genes.
The \syn~side was therefore renormalized to the retained-gene pool before any category comparison, dividing each \syn~relative value by the mean over detected genes that are kept in \syna, so that retained \syn~genes again average 1 and are matched to \syna; the genome-wide fold-change distributions and the per-gene impact-class comparison retained the original full-pool normalization, whereas every per-category share used this deletion-corrected basis.
For each category the mRNA-pool share in each organism and its change, the median fold and absolute change, and a two-sided Mann--Whitney $U$ test of $\log_{10}$ fold change against all remaining genes were reported for categories with at least three detected genes (sheet Function\_categories of \texttt{genome\_reduction.xlsx}).  % script: 09_Compare_RNA_Protein.py

\subsubsection{Protein-to-transcript ratio and macromolecular-complex abundance}

Post-transcriptional regulation was summarized per gene by the protein-to-transcript ratio (PTR), defined as relIPM divided by relTPM within an organism, with its cross-organism change equal to the iPM fold change divided by the TPM fold change.
PTR is a steady-state proxy for translation efficiency and is explicitly not a ribosome-profiling translation-efficiency measurement.
Ribosomal proteins were excluded from every PTR analysis because their iPM is biased by the protein-digestion workflow, and PTR was examined both as a transcript-against-protein fold-change scatter and as a distribution of $\log_{10}$ PTR fold change per Secondary function, with each category of at least three proteins tested by two-sided Mann--Whitney $U$ against the non-ribosomal baseline.
The assembled abundance of two complexes whose subunits are all protein-coding, RNA polymerase (RNAP) and the degradosome, was estimated from a limiting-subunit rule whereby surplus subunits are turned over, so that the complex copy number tracks its lowest stoichiometry-adjusted component (the minimum over subunits).
Core RNAP was estimated as the minimum of \textit{rpoA}/2, \textit{rpoC}, and \textit{rpoB} (the $\alpha_2\beta\beta'$ core, with $\sigma$ factors excluded), and the degradosome as the minimum of \textit{rny}, \textit{rnjA}, and \textit{yhaM}\,+\,\textit{rnr}.
Each estimate was computed in both relTPM and relIPM for each organism, the \syna/\syn~ratio being the interpretable cross-organism quantity, and related transcription and RNA-turnover capacity to \syna's longer cell cycle (sheet Summary\_stats of \texttt{genome\_reduction.xlsx}).  % script: 10_Compare_Ptn.py
