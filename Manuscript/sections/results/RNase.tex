\subsection{Pervasive RNA processing truncates transcripts with a 3' erosion bias}

Alongside each intact operon transcript, PacBio sequencing recovered many shorter isoforms; when an isoform's variable end falls inside a coding sequence, it records post-transcriptional processing rather than an alternative promoter or terminator.
Two retained genes illustrate the opposite polarities of this erosion.
At the neopullulanase gene \locus{0178}, 3$'$ erosion dominated the 85 operon-contained isoforms: unprocessed transcripts carried 65.5\% of the reads, while among the processed species 49 were truncated only at the 3$'$ end and 27 only at the 5$'$ end (Fig.~\ref{fig:rnase}b).
At the leucyl-aminopeptidase gene \gene{lap}{0154} the polarity was reversed: of its 134 isoforms, unprocessed transcripts carried 49.7\% of the reads, and 92 were truncated only at the 5$'$ end behind a shared 3$'$ terminator against only 29 at the 3$'$ end (Fig.~\ref{fig:rnase}c).
The \locus{0178} 3$'$ terminus folds into a canonical intrinsic terminator (Fig.~\ref{fig:rnase}d), yet erosion proceeds through it: RNase~R loads on the single-stranded poly-U overhang and degrades processively through the base-paired stem~\cite{hossain_how_2016}, so a stable 3$'$ structure does not by itself protect against 3$'\rightarrow$5$'$ erosion.

This processing was pervasive and strongly 3$'$-biased.
Each of the 20{,}885 well-supported isoforms ($\geq$10 reads) was classified by whether each end lay inside a coding sequence (a processing event) or in an intergenic region (a promoter or terminator).
Only 8.3\% of isoforms, carrying 27.0\% of the reads, had both ends intergenic and so were unprocessed primary transcripts (Fig.~\ref{fig:rnase}e).
Among the processed majority the asymmetry was pronounced: isoforms eroded only at the 3$'$ end made up 30.3\% of isoforms but 47.5\% of reads, against 19.5\% and 9.2\% for those eroded only at the 5$'$ end, so 5$'$ erosion carried just 0.40 times the read weight of 3$'$ erosion.
The same imbalance appeared in reading frames: 42.2\% of the open reading frames contained within an isoform kept a start codon but had lost their stop codon to 3$'$ truncation.

The directionality implicates the cell's exoribonucleases.
\syn~retains a compact ribonuclease complement (Table~\ref{tbl:rnase_syn1_syn3a}): three endoribonucleases (RNase~III, RNase~Y, RNase~M5), the 5$'\rightarrow$3$'$ exoribonucleases RNase~J1 and J2, and the 3$'\rightarrow$5$'$ exoribonucleases RNase~R and YhaM.
Endonucleolytic cleavage by RNase~III or RNase~Y opens internal sites, and the fragments are trimmed exonucleolytically from their new ends (Fig.~\ref{fig:rnase}f)~\cite{durand_rnases_2018}.
Two asymmetries between the directions can account for the bias toward intragenic 3$'$ ends.
First, they initiate differently: RNase~R can begin at an accessible 3$'$ end such as a terminator poly-U tail, whereas RNase~J1 requires a 5$'$-monophosphate end left by a prior cut and cannot initiate on an intact triphosphorylated 5$'$ end.
Second, the processive enzymes differ in abundance: RNase~J1 (92 copies, with RNase~J2 as cofactor) is more than twofold more abundant than RNase~R (36 copies, the only 3$'\rightarrow$5$'$ enzyme that reads through structure, since YhaM stalls at a stem), so 5$'$-eroded fragments are cleared rapidly while 3$'$-eroded fragments persist and accumulate.
Consistently, the same gene set encodes the trans-translation system, tmRNA (\gene{ssrA}{0158}) and SmpB (\gene{smpB}{0776}), which rescues ribosomes stalled on exactly the stop-codon-less messages 3$'$ erosion produces~\cite{janssen_tmrna_2012}, yet is itself low in abundance (SmpB at 14 copies), so this rescue capacity may also be limited.

RNA processing also remodels co-expression within an operon.
The ATP-synthase operon (\locus{0789}--\locus{0797}), transcribed as one reverse-strand unit with no internal terminator, split into two non-overlapping isoform populations meeting at the $\alpha$-subunit gene \gene{atpA}{0792} (Fig.~\ref{fig:rnase}g).
A 5$'$ population covered the F$_0$ subunits and $\delta$ (\locus{0793}--\locus{0796}) and ended at \gene{atpA}{0792}, while a 3$'$ population began there and ran through the F$_1$ subunits (\locus{0789}--\locus{0791}).
Read depth fell from about 9{,}000 to roughly half across the boundary, the signature of an internal endonucleolytic cut at \gene{atpA}{0792} that the segmentation resolved as two adjoining blocks.
The cut was assigned to RNase~III by homology to the orthologous \textit{B. subtilis} \gene{atpA}{0792}, which carries two characterised RNase~III sites~\cite{taggart_high-resolution_2025}; transferred onto the \syn~transcript, each site fell within a local stem, although the extended stem pairing the two cuts in \textit{B. subtilis} was not conserved (Methods).
A single primary transcript is thereby processed into separately decaying 5$'$ and 3$'$ blocks that can set the membrane F$_0$ and soluble F$_1$ sectors on different post-transcriptional fates~\cite{redko_minimal_2013}.
