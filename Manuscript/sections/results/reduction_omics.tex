\subsection{Minimization reallocates transcription toward translation at the expense of central metabolism}

Minimization removed not only genes but a share of the expression budget.
Of the 911 \syn~loci, 418 are absent from \syna (382 protein-coding, 33 pseudogenes, 3 structural RNAs), together carrying 21.8\% of the messenger-RNA pool and 22.3\% of the proteome in \syn (Methods); nearly half the genes thus held only about a fifth of the output, so the removed genes were well below average in expression.
Their most transcribed members, the yeast-vector marker \gene{lacZ}{0915}, the pyruvate-dehydrogenase subunits \gene{pdhA}{0225} and \gene{pdhB}{0226}, and alanine dehydrogenase \gene{ald}{0059}, are all dispensable in rich medium~\cite{breuer_essential_2019}.
Because relative units sum to a fixed total, excising a fifth of the output frees an equal fraction for retained genes, so the comparisons below are mean-normalized to the retained-gene pool, leaving an unchanged gene reading as no change (Methods).

Within this retained pool, the relative expression landscape was largely conserved across minimization.
Per-gene relative transcript levels in \syn~and \syna~correlated at Pearson $r = 0.84$ on $\log_{10}$ relative TPM ($n = 443$ protein-coding genes), and the proteome was conserved to a similar degree ($r = 0.87$ on $\log_{10}$ relative iPM, $n = 423$), so the ancestral expression hierarchy was inherited largely intact at both layers. \cmt{EFU: put syn1~syn3A correlation plot in SI figure}
The reallocation described below therefore rode on this conserved backdrop as a small number of large, discrete shifts rather than a genome-wide drift, the clearest being the collapse of the decapitated nucleoid protein HupA (Fig.~\ref{fig:reduction_operons}) and the rise of the ribosomal-protein operon dissected here.

The freed capacity was taken up overwhelmingly by the translation machinery rather than spread across functions (Fig.~\ref{fig:reduction_omics}a).
Translation, already the largest consumer in \syn, rose from 53.1\% to 64.9\% of the retained messenger-RNA pool (an 11.8-point gain over its 131 genes), the largest increases all ribosomal proteins (\gene{rpsK}{0646}, \gene{rplO}{0653}, \gene{rplX}{0660}, \gene{rplN}{0661}, each gaining 4.3 to 6.0 relative units).
The gain came at metabolism's expense: Central Carbon Metabolism fell from 17.0\% to 10.7\% (a 6.2-point drop over 49 genes; median fold change 0.66, two-sided Mann--Whitney $p = 1.3\times10^{-3}$), with smaller losses in Membrane Transport (0.80, $p = 0.016$) and Transcription (0.55, $p = 0.040$) (Fig.~\ref{fig:reduction_omics}b).
The reallocation was selective, not uniform: the two fusion-affected ribosomal-protein genes collapsed (\gene{rpsT}{0082}, \gene{rpsO}{0294}; Fig.~\ref{fig:reduction_operons}), and three structurally intact ones (\gene{rpmF}{0526}, \gene{rpmE}{0137}, \gene{rpsU}{0482}) also fell sharply in transcript (fold change 0.13, 0.01, and 0.04) despite unchanged context.

Two protein-only machines moved in opposite directions, estimated from their limiting subunit (Fig.~\ref{fig:reduction_omics}c).
Core RNA polymerase (minimum of \textit{rpoA}/2, \textit{rpoC}, \textit{rpoB}) fell to a fold change of 0.65 in transcript and 0.79 in protein (35\% and 21\% reductions), whereas the RNase~Y-based degradosome~\cite{redko_minimal_2013} (minimum of \textit{rny}, \textit{rnjA}, \textit{yhaM}\,+\,\textit{rnr}) rose to 1.68 and 1.36 (68\% and 36\% increases); lower transcription with higher RNA-turnover capacity is coherent with \syna's longer cell cycle, roughly 105~min against 60~min for \syn~\cite{pelletier_genetic_2021}.
The central-metabolic decline was coordinated across the energy-yielding pathways at both layers: in glycolysis \gene{eno}{0213}, \gene{pgk}{0606}, and \gene{pyk}{0221} fell to transcript/protein fold changes of 0.37/0.38, 0.35/0.44, and 0.51/0.34; the pyruvate-dehydrogenase E1 subunits \gene{pdhA}{0225} and \gene{pdhB}{0226} were deleted outright while the retained E2 \gene{pdhC}{0227} dropped to 0.45/0.34; and the acetate and lactate steps \gene{pta}{0229}, \gene{ackA}{0230}, and \gene{ldh}{0475} fell to 0.27/0.26, 0.19/0.44, and 0.66/0.70.
This concerted downgrade predicts lower ATP and GTP output in \syna, a prediction awaiting an explicit metabolic-flux comparison.

The reallocation toward translation was dominated by a single transcription unit (Fig.~\ref{fig:reduction_omics}d).
The $\sim$11~kb ribosomal-protein operon \locus{0652}--\locus{0672}, a 21-gene polycistron from one promoter with no internal terminator, rose from 12\% of the \syn~coding messenger-RNA pool to 34\% in \syna, so over a third of all coding transcription in the minimal cell issued from this one operon.
Full-length $\sim$11~kb reads were rare at the read-length limit, but continuous coverage with no internal terminator marked it as one unit, while a steep 5$'$ polarity gradient and a sharp depth step indicated internal endonucleolytic processing.
The flanking deletions, which removed \gene{dhaK}{0673} through \locus{0677}, brought a co-directional four-tRNA operon (\locus{0678}--\locus{0681}) to within 772~bp of its start, against 7{,}193~bp in \syn, yet the operon's own promoter was retained 179~bp from the nearest deletion.
Despite this adjacency the two were not co-transcribed (no \syna~direct-RNA read spanned the junction, 0 of 3084; intervening short-read coverage 1.2\% of the flanking depth; Fig.~\ref{fig:reduction_omics}e), so the operon's dominance rested on its own retained promoter and the pool-level reallocation, not on read-through from the relocated tRNA genes.
