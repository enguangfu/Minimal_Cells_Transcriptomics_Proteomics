\subsection{Minimization reallocates transcription toward translation at the expense of central metabolism}

Minimization removed not only genes but a substantial share of the expression budget.
Of the 911 \syn~loci, 418 are absent from \syna: 382 protein-coding genes, 33 pseudogenes, and 3 structural RNAs.
In \syn~these deleted loci together accounted for 21.8\% of the messenger-RNA pool and 22.3\% of the proteome by mass-spectrometry intensity (Methods).
Their most highly transcribed members were the yeast-vector marker \gene{lacZ}{0915}, the pyruvate-dehydrogenase subunits \gene{pdhA}{0225} and \gene{pdhB}{0226}, and alanine dehydrogenase \gene{ald}{0059}, all dispensable for growth in rich medium~\cite{breuer_essential_2019}.
Because relative transcript and protein units each sum to a fixed total within an organism, excising roughly a fifth of the coding output frees an equivalent fraction of the pool for the retained genes; the cross-organism comparisons below therefore use mean-normalized units renormalized to the retained-gene pool, so that a gene whose true abundance is unchanged reads as no change despite this freed capacity (Methods).

The freed capacity was taken up overwhelmingly by the translation machinery rather than spread evenly across functions (Fig.~\ref{fig:reduction_omics}a).
The Translation category, already the largest consumer of transcription in \syn, rose from 53.1\% to 64.9\% of the retained messenger-RNA pool, an 11.8-percentage-point gain across its 131 genes, and the largest single increases were all ribosomal proteins (\gene{rpsK}{0646}, \gene{rplO}{0653}, \gene{rplX}{0660}, and \gene{rplN}{0661}, each gaining 4.3 to 6.0 relative units).
This gain came at the expense of metabolism: Central Carbon Metabolism fell from 17.0\% to 10.7\%, a 6.2-point drop across 49 genes (median fold change 0.66, two-sided Mann--Whitney $p = 1.3\times10^{-3}$), with smaller but significant losses in Membrane Transport (median 0.80, $p = 0.016$) and Transcription (median 0.55, $p = 0.040$) (Fig.~\ref{fig:reduction_omics}b).
The reallocation was not uniform, however: the two ribosomal-protein genes whose original promoter was deleted and replaced by a weak fused promoter instead collapsed (\gene{rpsT}{0082}, \gene{rpsO}{0294}; Fig.~\ref{fig:reduction_operons}), so the shift is a redistribution of the pool rather than a uniform up-regulation of every translation gene.

The reallocation toward translation was dominated by a single transcription unit.
The $\sim$11~kb ribosomal-protein and translation-machinery operon \locus{0652}--\locus{0672}, a 21-gene polycistron transcribed from one promoter without an internal terminator, rose from 12\% of the \syn~coding messenger-RNA pool to 34\% in \syna, so that over a third of all coding transcription in the minimal cell issued from this one operon (Fig.~\ref{fig:reduction_omics}d).
Full-length $\sim$11~kb reads were rare at the read-length limit of the long-read data, but continuous nonzero coverage and the absence of any internal terminator marked the locus as one unit, while a steep 5$'$ polarity gradient with a sharp depth step partway along indicated internal endonucleolytic processing of the polycistron.
The flanking deletions, which removed \gene{dhaK}{0673} through \locus{0677}, brought a co-directional four-tRNA operon (\locus{0678}--\locus{0681}, transfer RNAs for threonine, valine, glutamate, and asparagine) to within 772~bp of the operon's start, against 7193~bp in \syn, yet the operon's own promoter was retained 179~bp from the nearest deletion.
Despite this new adjacency the two operons were not co-transcribed: no \syna~direct-RNA read spanned the junction (0 of 3084) and the intervening sequence was transcriptionally silent in short-read coverage at 1.2\% of the flanking operon depth (Fig.~\ref{fig:reduction_omics}e).
The dominance of this operon therefore rested on its own retained promoter together with the pool-level reallocation, not on read-through from the relocated tRNA genes.

The opposing movement of two protein-only machines, estimated from their limiting (lowest-stoichiometry) subunit, accompanied this reweighting.
Core RNA polymerase, taken as the minimum of \textit{rpoA}/2, \textit{rpoC}, and \textit{rpoB}, fell to a fold change of 0.65 in transcript and 0.79 in protein (a 35\% and a 21\% reduction), whereas the RNase~Y-based degradosome~\cite{redko_minimal_2013}, the endoribonuclease around which the minimal degradosome is organized and taken as the minimum of \textit{rny}, \textit{rnjA}, and \textit{yhaM}\,+\,\textit{rnr}, rose to 1.68 in transcript and 1.36 in protein (a 68\% and a 36\% increase) (Fig.~\ref{fig:reduction_omics}c).
Lower transcription capacity together with higher RNA-turnover capacity is coherent with the slower growth of \syna, whose cell cycle is roughly 105~min against 60~min for \syn~\cite{pelletier_genetic_2021}.

The decline of Central Carbon Metabolism was coordinated across the energy-yielding pathways at both the transcript and the protein level (Fig.~\ref{fig:reduction_omics}c).
In glycolysis the enzymes \gene{eno}{0213}, \gene{pgk}{0606}, and \gene{pyk}{0221} fell to transcript and protein fold changes of 0.37/0.38, 0.35/0.44, and 0.51/0.34; the pyruvate-dehydrogenase E1 subunits \gene{pdhA}{0225} and \gene{pdhB}{0226} were deleted outright while the retained E2 subunit \gene{pdhC}{0227} dropped to 0.45/0.34; and the acetate- and lactate-producing steps \gene{pta}{0229}, \gene{ackA}{0230}, and \gene{ldh}{0475} fell to 0.27/0.26, 0.19/0.44, and 0.66/0.70.
A concerted downgrade of the glycolytic, acetate, and lactate enzymes at both layers predicts a correspondingly lower ATP and GTP output in \syna, a prediction that remains to be tested against an explicit metabolic-flux comparison.
