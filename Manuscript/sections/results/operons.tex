\subsection{Full-length PacBio sequencing resolves 459 operons and their transcription signals in \syn}

To resolve the operons in the synthetic bacterium \jsyn~(\syn), full-length transcripts were captured by PacBio HiFi sequencing of its cDNA library.
After full-length non-chimeric (FLNC) recovery and filtering, 2.6~M high-quality reads gave an average sequencing depth of 2.1~k (Methods).
To remove residual end noise, reads sharing transcript ends within 10~bp were clustered into 267~k isoform clusters, each a distinct transcript species with sharply defined boundaries.

Operons were segmented from these clusters in three stages (Methods).
The 4~k clusters supported by at least 50 reads were grouped into candidate operons by containment clustering, yielding 313 initial operons.
Adjacent same-strand operons that overlapped, or flanked a same-strand gene in a coverage hole, were tested for read-level co-transcription and merged where continuous PacBio coverage bridged the junction, collapsing the set to 275.
Finally, genes left uncovered were rescued from spanning reads, the two highly structured rRNA operons were added directly, and remaining isolated genes were assigned as monocistronic operons.
This produced a final map of 459 operons covering all 911 annotated \syn~genes on the sense strand.

An operon spanned a median 1{,}650~bp (mean 2{,}049~bp) and a mean of 2.0 sense genes (Fig.~\ref{fig:operon}b,c), with a long polycistronic tail culminating in the $\sim$11~kb ribosomal-protein supercluster (\gene{rpsJ}{0672}$\rightarrow$\gene{secY}{0652}, 20 ribosomal proteins plus SecY), the largest operon in the genome.
Beyond their sense genes, 69 operons (15.0\%) also enclosed a gene transcribed from the opposite strand and nine covered only antisense or intergenic regions, so operon boundaries frequently captured antisense transcription as well.

Transcription start and termination sites (TSS, TTS) were read from the 127 canonical operons whose boundaries both fall in intergenic regions, where the ends reflect genuine regulatory positions rather than processed termini (Methods).
Upstream of the TSS the bacterial $-10$ element was clearly recovered, with the \texttt{TANAAT} hexamer matching 87 of 127 operons (69\%) and the extended \texttt{TNNTANAAT} motif 52 (41\%), whereas the $-35$ region carried no fixed hexamer and was only broadly AT-rich (Fig.~\ref{fig:operon}d).
At the TTS, rho-independent terminators~\cite{kingsford_rapid_2007} were found in 97 of 127 operons (76\%), the mapped TTS lying a median of 1~nt beyond the poly-U tract (98\% within 10~nt), with the canonical architecture of a stem-loop (median stem 8~bp, loop 4~nt) and a uridine-rich tail (median 6~nt), the stems enriched in G+C against the AT-rich genome (43\% versus 24\%) (Fig.~\ref{fig:operon}d--g).
These conventional $-10$ and intrinsic-terminator elements indicate that the reduced genome retains a standard transcription-signal architecture, though the assignment is limited to operons with intergenic boundaries.

The operon structure and gene co-expression we resolved for the division and cell wall (\textit{dcw}) cluster \gene{mraZ}{0525}--\locus{0520}, together with its upstream co-transcribed genes \gene{rpmF}{0526} and \locus{0527}, in \syn~support previous studies of the cell-division mechanism.
Full-length PacBio isoforms segmented the entire region into two operons, a 5$'$ unit \locus{0527}--\gene{rpmF}{0526}--\gene{mraZ}{0525}--\gene{mraW}{0524}--\gene{ftsA}{0523} and a 3$'$ unit \gene{ftsZ}{0522}--\gene{sepF}{0521}--\locus{0520}, each with its own promoter and \threeprime~intrinsic terminator.
In addition, a weak internal promoter re-initiating transcription at \gene{mraZ}{0525} was evident in the ONT reads of both \syn~and \syna~(Fig.~\ref{fig:si-dcw}).
Removing \locus{0527} in \synthreezero~would also remove this promoter and thus suppress the downstream \textit{mra} genes, offering a transcription-level rationale for why \locus{0527} had to be added back to build a minimal cell with normal morphology~\cite{pelletier_genetic_2021}.
Consistently, restoring \locus{0527} and the \gene{ftsZ}{0522}--\gene{sepF}{0521}--\locus{0520} cluster back to \syna~recovered \syn-like transcription across the \textit{dcw} cluster in both the ONT and Illumina reads.
Across \gene{mraW}{0524}--\locus{0520}, both the mRNA and protein fold changes between \syn~and \syna~stayed within 0.5 to 2.5 with a median near unity, indicating comparable expression in the two cells.
